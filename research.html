The Research

The science behind the healing.

Curious how these approaches work? Below are plain-language explanations and links to research from trusted medical institutions and peer-reviewed studies, so you can explore the evidence for yourself.

Educational information only. This page is not medical advice and does not endorse any treatment for your situation. Always talk with a licensed provider, and never stop or change medication without consulting them first.

Stellate Ganglion Block (SGB) Emerging

A quick injection of local anesthetic into a bundle of nerves in the neck that helps regulate the body's "fight-or-flight" response. For some people with PTSD, calming these nerves appears to turn down chronic hyperarousal, anxiety, and sleep problems.

Trusted overview

  • Stellate Ganglion Block: What It Is, Uses & RisksCleveland Clinic

Research studies

  • Effect of SGB on PTSD Symptoms: A Randomized Clinical Trial (active-duty military)JAMA Psychiatry, 2020
  • SGB for PTSD, Depression, and Anxiety — evidence reviewNCBI Bookshelf (NIH)

Ketamine Therapy Emerging

Given in low, medically supervised doses, ketamine can rapidly ease depression and, in growing research, PTSD symptoms — often working within hours when other treatments haven't. (A related medicine, esketamine, is FDA-approved for treatment-resistant depression.)

Research studies

  • Ketamine as a Treatment for Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder: A ReviewPubMed Central (NIH)
  • Ketamine for chronic pain, depression, and PTSD in Veterans: Meta-Analysis & Systematic ReviewPubMed Central (NIH)
  • Ketamine Treatment for Depression: A ReviewPubMed Central (NIH)

EMDR Therapy Strong

Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing uses guided eye movements while recalling a traumatic memory, helping the brain reprocess and "file away" the memory so it loses its emotional charge. The VA and Department of Defense list EMDR as a best-practice PTSD treatment.

Trusted overview

  • EMDR Therapy: What It Is, Procedure & EffectivenessCleveland Clinic
  • EMDR among recommended PTSD treatmentsVA — National Center for PTSD

Research studies

  • EMDR for PTSD: meta-analytic evidence for specific treatment effectsPubMed Central (NIH)

Mindfulness & MBSR Growing

Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) teaches present-moment awareness and acceptance rather than avoidance. For veterans, it can reduce PTSD and depression symptoms and improve sleep and emotional regulation.

Trusted overview

  • Mind & Body Approaches for Stress and Anxiety: What the Science SaysNational Institutes of Health — NCCIH

Research studies

  • Multisite Randomized Controlled Trial of MBSR for PTSDPubMed Central (NIH)
  • MBSR effects on depression, PTSD & mindfulness in veterans: Systematic Review & Meta-AnalysisPubMed Central (NIH)

Trauma-Sensitive Yoga Emerging

Gentle, body-aware yoga helps veterans reconnect with the body, calm the nervous system, and build a sense of safety and control. In one notable trial, over half of women with treatment-resistant PTSD no longer met criteria after yoga.

Research studies

  • Meditation and Yoga for PTSD: A Meta-Analytic Review of Randomized Controlled TrialsPubMed Central (NIH)
  • Yoga as an adjunctive treatment for PTSD: a randomized controlled trialPubMed (NIH)

Tai Chi & Qigong Growing

Often called "meditation in motion," these slow, flowing movement practices combine gentle exercise with breath and focus. Research links them to reduced anxiety and depression and improved mood and well-being.

Trusted overview

  • The Health Benefits of Tai ChiHarvard Health Publishing — Harvard Medical School

Research studies

  • Qigong and Tai Chi for Mood RegulationFOCUS (American Psychiatric Association)

Acupuncture Emerging

Thin needles placed at specific points appear to calm the body's stress response. A recent trial in combat veterans found meaningful PTSD symptom reduction, with effects comparable to established therapies.

Research studies

  • Acupuncture for Combat-Related PTSD: A Randomized Clinical TrialPubMed (NIH)
  • Acupuncture for PTSD: A Systematic Review of Randomized Controlled TrialsPubMed Central (NIH)
  • Needle Acupuncture for PTSD: A Systematic ReviewRAND Corporation

Massage Therapy Emerging

Therapeutic touch can lower the stress hormone cortisol and ease muscle tension, helping reduce anxiety, stress, and hyperarousal as a complement to other care.

Trusted overview

  • Massage Therapy: What You Need To KnowNational Institutes of Health — NCCIH
  • Massage helps anxiety, depression and stressMayo Clinic Health System

Equine-Assisted Therapy Preliminary

Working with horses builds trust, emotional awareness, and calm. Horses respond honestly to a person's emotional state, giving veterans real-time, non-judgmental feedback. Early evidence is promising, with larger trials still underway.

Research studies

  • Equine-assisted interventions for veterans with PTSD: a systematic reviewPubMed Central (NIH)
  • Equine-Assisted Therapy for Veterans with PTSD: Manual Development & Preliminary FindingsPubMed Central (NIH)

Psychiatric Service Dogs Growing

Trained service dogs help veterans manage anxiety, nightmares, and flashbacks, and support social reconnection. A major NIH-funded trial found veterans with service dogs had markedly lower PTSD symptom severity.

Trusted overview & research

  • Service dogs may reduce PTSD symptoms for military members and veteransNational Institutes of Health (NIH)
  • Service Dogs for Veterans With PTSD: A Nonrandomized Controlled TrialJAMA Network Open
  • Service Dogs Lower Severity of PTSD Symptoms in VeteransU.S. Department of Veterans Affairs

Art Therapy Emerging

Creating art helps veterans express what's hard to put into words, process trauma memories, and reduce avoidance and hyper-vigilance — a valuable complement to talk-based treatment.

Research studies

  • A Review of Art Therapy Among Military Service Members and Veterans with PTSDJournal of Military and Veterans' Health
  • A conceptual framework for a neurophysiological basis of art therapy for PTSDPubMed Central (NIH)

Music Therapy Emerging

Making or engaging with music can ease intrusive symptoms, anxiety, and isolation, and build connection. Group drumming and music-instruction programs have shown encouraging results with veterans.

Research studies

  • Music-instruction intervention for PTSD: a randomized pilot study (VA Medical Center)PubMed Central (NIH)

Float Therapy (Flotation-REST) Emerging

Floating effortlessly in warm, Epsom-salt water in a quiet, low-stimulation environment gives the nervous system a deep rest. Studies show single sessions can meaningfully reduce anxiety, with the largest effects in the most anxious individuals.

Research studies

  • Short-term anxiolytic and antidepressant effect of Floatation-RESTPLOS ONE
  • Randomized controlled trial of floatation-REST in anxious and depressed individualsPubMed (NIH)
